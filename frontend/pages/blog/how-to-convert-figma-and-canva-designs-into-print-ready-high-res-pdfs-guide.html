How to convert Figma and Canva designs into print-ready high-res PDFs

By PDFjin Content Team • Oct 11, 2026 • 6 min read
Share:

Mastering Print-Ready PDFs from Figma and Canva

The core challenge when moving designs from web-native platforms like Figma and Canva to professional print is converting RGB color spaces to CMYK, ensuring high image resolution, and correctly applying bleeds and crop marks. This guide provides precise, actionable steps to ensure your digital designs meet professional print requirements, minimizing common color shifts and resolution issues.

Preparing Figma Designs for Print

Figma primarily operates in an sRGB color environment. Understanding its export limitations and best practices is crucial for achieving accurate print output.

Configure Your Document Dimensions

  • Set Frame Size: Design your frame using the final print dimensions plus the required bleed. For example, an 8.5x11 inch flyer with a 0.125-inch bleed on all sides needs a 8.75x11.25 inch frame.
  • Add Bleed Guides: Manually draw guides within your Figma frame to visualize the bleed area. This helps ensure critical elements are not too close to the edge.

Export Settings for High-Res PDF

When your design is complete, select the frame you wish to export and open the Export panel in the right sidebar.

  • Format: Choose "PDF".
  • Include Bleed: Check this box and input your specific bleed value (e.g., 0.125in). This ensures elements extend past the trim edge.
  • Flatten PDF: For complex designs with numerous layers and effects, flattening can prevent rendering inconsistencies in print. However, it limits post-export editing.
  • Image Quality: Figma strives to maintain original image quality. Ensure all raster images (photos) used within your Figma design are already high resolution (preferably 300 DPI at their intended print size).

Color Profile Considerations in Figma

Figma's native sRGB color profile will be converted to CMYK for commercial printing. This conversion can lead to subtle color shifts.

  • Soft Proofing: Be aware that colors may appear different in print than on screen. If precise color matching is critical, consider a test print or use an external tool like Adobe Acrobat Pro for CMYK preview.
  • Vector vs. Raster: Prioritize vector elements (shapes, text) in Figma as they scale perfectly without pixelation. Ensure any embedded raster images (photos, textures) are 300 DPI or higher to avoid blurriness.

Exporting Print-Ready PDFs from Canva

Canva streamlines many print settings, but understanding its specific download options is key to avoiding common pitfalls.

Design Setup in Canva

  • Create Design: Begin with the correct custom dimensions, including any necessary bleed. Canva often provides print product templates that already account for bleed areas.
  • Show Print Bleed: Navigate to File → View settings → Show print bleed. This adds a dotted line within your canvas. Ensure all background colors and images extend fully to the edge of the document, past this bleed line.

Download Options for Print

Once your design is ready, click "Share" in the top right corner, then select "Download".

  • File Type: Select "PDF Print". This option is specifically optimized for high-quality printing.
  • Flatten PDF: Canva automatically flattens designs when using PDF Print, which helps prevent rendering issues on the printer's end.
  • Crop Marks and Bleed: Check this box. This adds the essential trim and bleed marks that your commercial printer needs to cut the document accurately.
  • Color Profile: If you have Canva Pro, choose "CMYK (best for professional printing)" when prompted. This is a critical step for accurate color reproduction. Standard Canva accounts typically default to an RGB to CMYK conversion.

Image Resolution in Canva

While Canva handles much of the export process, the quality of your source images remains paramount.

  • Upload High-Res Assets: Always upload images that are 300 DPI or higher for best print results. Canva cannot magically improve a low-resolution image.
  • Zoom Check: Zoom into your design in Canva to 100% or 200%. If your images appear pixelated or blurry on screen at this magnification, they will almost certainly print poorly.

Post-Export Checks and Refinements

After exporting your PDF, a few final checks can prevent costly print errors and ensure your design meets professional standards.

Verify PDF Specifications

Parameter Print Standard Verification Method
Color Profile CMYK Open in Adobe Acrobat Pro or similar; use the Output Preview tool.
Resolution 300 DPI for images Zoom in significantly to inspect images; check document properties if available.
Bleed Margin 0.125 inches (standard) Visually confirm background elements extend past the crop marks.
Font Embedding All fonts embedded Check PDF Document Properties → Fonts to ensure no missing fonts.

Managing File Size

High-resolution print PDFs, especially those with many images, can be quite large. If your printer has file size limits for uploads, optimization may be necessary.

  • Compression Tools: Utilize PDF compression tools to reduce file size without significant loss of quality for print. Many online tools offer this, or quickly compress the PDF file before sending it to your printer.

Conclusion

Converting web-native designs from Figma and Canva into truly print-ready, high-resolution PDFs requires careful attention to dimensions, bleeds, color profiles, and image resolution. By diligently following these platform-specific guidelines and conducting thorough post-export checks, you can consistently achieve high-quality print results.
