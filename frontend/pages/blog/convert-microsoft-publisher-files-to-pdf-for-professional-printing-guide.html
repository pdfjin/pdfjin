Convert Microsoft Publisher files to PDF for professional printing

By PDFjin Content Team • Aug 09, 2026 • 6 min read
Share:

Why Publisher Files Fall Short for Professional Printing

Microsoft Publisher files (PUB) are designed for desktop publishing, but they are generally unsuitable for direct submission to professional print shops. This is primarily due to their proprietary nature and lack of consistent asset embedding. Print service providers rarely use Publisher and often cannot open, interpret, or correctly render PUB files, leading to font substitutions, image placement errors, and color shifts.

The Superiority of PDF for Print Workflows

PDF (Portable Document Format) is the industry standard for professional printing because it encapsulates all necessary document elements. This includes fonts, images, and graphics, ensuring that the file displays identically across different systems and software. A properly created print-ready PDF guarantees consistency from design to final output.

Method 1 Convert Directly within Microsoft Publisher

The most reliable way to convert your Publisher document to a print-ready PDF is directly within the application itself. Publisher provides robust PDF export options critical for professional printing.

Step-by-Step Save As PDF

Follow these steps to generate a high-quality PDF from your Publisher file:

  1. Open your Publisher document. Ensure all design elements, text, and images are finalized.
  2. Navigate to the File tab on the ribbon.
  3. Select Export from the left-hand menu.
  4. Click Create PDF/XPS Document.
  5. In the "Publish as PDF or XPS" dialog box, choose a save location and enter a file name.
  6. Before clicking "Publish", ensure you select the correct optimization options. This is crucial for print quality.

Critical Print Settings for PDF Conversion

When in the "Publish as PDF or XPS" dialog, click the Options button to access advanced PDF settings:

  • Publish Options Select High Quality Printing or Commercial Press. Avoid "Standard publishing" or "Minimum size" for professional printing, as these options reduce quality.
  • Print Options Ensure "Bleeds marks" and "Crop marks" are checked if your design extends to the edge of the page. This provides crucial guides for the printer.
  • Include non-printing guides Uncheck this option to prevent design aids from appearing in your final PDF.
  • Embed fonts Always ensure this option is selected. It guarantees that all fonts used in your document are included within the PDF, preventing font substitution issues.
  • Image compression For high quality printing, choose "Do not compress" or "Commercial press (300 dpi)". Avoid lower DPI settings that could pixelate images.
  • Color model Where available, ensure the output color model is set to CMYK. This matches the four-color printing process.

After configuring these settings, click OK, then Publish to create your PDF.

Verifying Output

Always open the generated PDF in a professional PDF viewer (like Adobe Acrobat Reader) and thoroughly check it. Look for correct fonts, image resolution, color accuracy, and proper bleeds or crop marks. This final verification step can prevent costly reprints.

Method 2 Use a Virtual PDF Printer

If you encounter issues with Publisher's native export or require more specific control, using a virtual PDF printer driver can be an alternative. Windows 10/11 includes "Microsoft Print to PDF" as a built-in option, or you can install a third-party PDF printer.

  1. With your Publisher document open, navigate to File > Print.
  2. From the printer dropdown menu, select Microsoft Print to PDF or your installed third-party PDF printer.
  3. Click Printer Properties or Preferences to adjust settings like page size, print quality, and possibly color management. Aim for "High Quality Print" or "Press Quality" profiles if available.
  4. Click Print. You will be prompted to save the PDF file to a chosen location.

While often effective, this method might offer fewer specific print-related controls compared to Publisher's dedicated "Export as PDF" options for bleeds or specific PDF/X standards.

Key PDF Settings for Professional Printing Success

Understanding these critical settings ensures your PDF is truly print-ready.

Bleed and Crop Marks

Bleed is the portion of your design that extends beyond the trim edge of the page. This prevents unsightly white edges if the trimming machine cuts slightly off. Crop marks indicate where the paper should be trimmed. Both are essential for professional-looking printed materials and should be included during PDF export if your design requires them.

Color Space CMYK Versus RGB

Digital screens use RGB (Red, Green, Blue) for color, while professional printing uses CMYK (Cyan, Magenta, Yellow, Key Black) inks. Converting your file to CMYK ensures that colors are accurately represented in print. If your Publisher file contains RGB images, Publisher's "High Quality Printing" or "Commercial Press" PDF export options will typically handle the CMYK conversion, but it's good practice to design in CMYK from the start.

Font Embedding

Fonts must be embedded in your PDF to ensure your text appears exactly as designed. Without embedding, the print shop's system might substitute fonts, leading to text reflow, incorrect spacing, or an unprofessional appearance. Always confirm full font embedding during conversion.

Image Resolution

For high-quality printing, images should have a resolution of at least 300 DPI (dots per inch) at their final printed size. Lower resolution images (e.g., 72 DPI common for web use) will appear pixelated or blurry when printed. Ensure all images in your Publisher document meet this standard before conversion.

Troubleshooting Common Post Conversion Issues

Even with careful conversion, issues can arise. Here's how to address frequent problems.

Incorrect Colors or Shifting

If colors appear different in the PDF than in Publisher, or on the printed output, review your CMYK conversion settings. Ensure you selected a print-specific PDF profile and that your original Publisher document's color settings were appropriate. Avoid generic "print" options that might default to RGB output.

Missing Fonts or Text Reflow

This is almost always due to incomplete font embedding. Go back to Publisher's PDF export options and confirm that "Embed fonts" was selected and successfully processed. If the problem persists, ensure you are using licensed fonts and that they are properly installed on your system.

Overly Large PDF Files

High-resolution images and lack of efficient compression can result in excessively large PDF files, which are slow to upload and process. If your print-ready PDF is too big, consider optimizing your PDF. You can quickly compress the PDF file using an online tool before sending it to the printer, reducing upload times without compromising print quality.
