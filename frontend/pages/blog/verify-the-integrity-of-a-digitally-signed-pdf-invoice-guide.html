Verify the integrity of a digitally signed PDF invoice

By PDFjin Content Team • Aug 10, 2026 • 6 min read
Share:

To verify the integrity of a digitally signed PDF invoice, open the document in a reputable PDF viewer like Adobe Acrobat Reader. The viewer will automatically evaluate the digital signature and display a clear status indicator, typically at the top of the document or within a dedicated signature panel. A valid signature confirms that the document originated from the stated signer and has not been altered since it was signed.

Understanding Digital Signature Essentials

Digital signatures are cryptographic mechanisms used to assure the authenticity, integrity, and non-repudiation of digital documents. Unlike scanned handwritten signatures, a digital signature binds a signer's identity to the document's content using a mathematical algorithm and a digital certificate.

  • Authenticity: Confirms the identity of the signer.
  • Integrity: Guarantees the document has not been tampered with after signing.
  • Non-Repudiation: Prevents the signer from falsely denying they signed the document.

When you digitally sign a PDF, a unique cryptographic hash of the document's content is generated and encrypted with your private key. Any subsequent change to the document, even a single character, will alter this hash, causing the signature validation to fail.

Step by Step Verification Using Adobe Acrobat Reader

Adobe Acrobat Reader is a widely used tool for signature validation due to its robust support for PKI standards. The process is straightforward and provides comprehensive details about the signature's status.

Access the Signature Panel

Upon opening a signed PDF, Adobe Acrobat Reader often displays a blue bar or a notification at the top indicating the signature status. Click on this notification or navigate to the "Signatures" panel, usually accessible from the left-hand sidebar.

Examine Signature Validity

The signature panel will list all digital signatures present in the document. For each signature, you will see an immediate status indicator:

  • Valid Signature: The signer's identity is verified, and the document has not been modified since signing.
  • Invalid Signature: The document has been altered, or the signature itself is corrupt.
  • Signature is Questionable: Issues with the signer's certificate or trust chain, but not necessarily document alteration.
  • Unknown Validity: Often due to missing certificate revocation information or untrusted root certificates.

View Signature Properties and Certificate Details

Right-click on the signature in the panel and select "Show Signature Properties." This dialog box provides granular details, including:

  • The signer's identity and certificate information.
  • The time and date of signing.
  • Information on whether the document was modified after signing and if so, what kind of modifications.
  • The chain of trust for the digital certificate, from the end-user certificate up to the Root Certificate Authority (CA).

Common Signature Validation Statuses

Understanding the different validation messages is crucial for discerning the true integrity of your invoice.

Status Meaning Action Required
Valid Signature Document integrity and signer identity confirmed. Proceed with confidence.
Signature Invalid Document was altered after signing, or signature data is corrupt. DO NOT trust document. Contact sender for verification.
Unknown Validity Cannot verify signer's certificate path or revocation status. Verify sender and certificate details manually. May require updating trust settings.
Signature is Questionable Certificate trust issues (e.g., expired, revoked, untrusted CA). Exercise caution. Investigate certificate trust.

What to Do if a Signature is Invalid

An invalid signature is a critical red flag. It indicates a breach of trust or integrity that requires immediate attention.

  • Do Not Trust the Document: Assume the invoice's content may have been compromised.
  • Contact the Sender Directly: Reach out via an independent, verified communication channel (not by replying to the potentially compromised email) to confirm the invoice's authenticity and request a new, properly signed document.
  • Examine Signature Properties: Look for details about "Changes to the document after signing." Most viewers can show you what changes were made.

It is paramount to investigate any invalidation thoroughly before processing payments or acting on the invoice's information. Maintaining document integrity is a cornerstone of secure financial transactions.

Underlying Technology A Brief Look

Digital signatures rely on a sophisticated framework called Public Key Infrastructure (PKI). This involves a pair of cryptographically linked keys a public key and a private key and digital certificates issued by trusted Certificate Authorities (CAs).

When a document is signed, a hash (a unique digital fingerprint) of its content is created. This hash is then encrypted using the signer's private key. To verify, the recipient's software uses the signer's public key (found in their digital certificate) to decrypt the hash. It then independently generates a hash of the received document. If both hashes match, the document's integrity is confirmed.
